Industrial-to-Residential (I-to-R) Conversion Masterclass

Master one of the most lucrative and legally complex procedures in Maharashtra real estate: converting industrial lands and closed factory sites into residential and commercial townships under Regulation 4.8.1. Understand the 5% land value premium, Labour Commissioner clearance, tiered public amenity surrender formulas, and in-situ FSI credits.

01 // EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Plain-Language Executive Summary

As cities expand, legacy manufacturing belts (such as Thane-Belapur, Pimpri-Chinchwad, Nashik, and Aurangabad) are routinely converted into high-density residential complexes. Regulation 4.8.1 establishes the statutory gateway for "I-to-R" (Industrial to Residential) conversions.

1. Statutory Pre-Conditions for I-to-R Conversion

  • Conversion Premium (Reg. 4.8.1(b)(i)): A mandatory conversion premium of 5% of the land value arrived at as per the Annual Statement of Rates (ASR / Ready Reckoner) without guidelines. This fee is split equally: 50% to the Planning Authority and 50% to the State Government.
  • Labour Commissioner NOC (Reg. 4.8.1(b)(ii)): For industries that are non-operational or slated for closure, an NOC from the Labour Commissioner, Maharashtra State, is mandatory, certifying that all legal dues to workers have been settled. Crucial Exemption: Raw open land in an industrial zone where an industry never existed does not require a Labour NOC.
  • Exclusion of Cotton Textile Mills: Lands of Cotton Textile Mills are expressly excluded from Regulation 4.8.1 (they are governed by special state mill land policies).
  • Exemption from Inclusive Housing (Reg. 4.8.1(b)(xvi)): The mandatory 20% Inclusive Housing scheme under Regulation 3.8 is expressly exempt for I-to-R conversions!

2. Mandatory Amenity Space Surrender Quotas (Reg. 4.8.1(b)(v–vi))

To prevent urban infrastructure overload, the developer must surrender unencumbered land to the Planning Authority for public amenities (schools, dispensaries, gardens, police posts):

Industrial Land Holding Area Public Amenity Surrender Requirement Special Alternative Option
Plots up to 1.0 Hectare (10,000 sq.m) 10% of land area Owner may provide 5% built-up space in lieu of land, preferably on the ground floor.
Plots between 1.0 Ha and 2.0 Hectares 10% of land area Must surrender physical unencumbered land with proper access and leveling.
Plots greater than 2.0 Hectares 10% for first 2.0 Ha
+ 15% for area above 2.0 Ha		 Graduated tiered land surrender free of cost to Planning Authority.

3. The 50% Unbuildable Rule & FSI Compensation (Reg. 4.8.1(b)(viii, xi))

  • 50% Green Reservation: At least 50% of the surrendered amenity land must be reserved by the Authority for strictly unbuildable recreational purposes (gardens, playgrounds, public parks). If the amenity space is under 1,000 sq.m, 100% of it must be kept unbuildable.
  • 100% In-Situ FSI or TDR Credit: The owner receives full compensatory FSI equivalent to the surrendered amenity area (or TDR under Regulation 11.2) for utilization on the remaining buildable land, subject to Table 6-A maximum building potential.
02 // STATUTORY RULE

Statutory Rule & Clause Breakdown

UDCPR Reg. 4.8.1 STATUTORY PROVISION

"4.8.1 Allowing Residential / Commercial Uses in Industrial Zone: The lands in the Industrial Zone in Development Plan, Regional Plan excluding the area of Cotton Textile Mills... may be utilised for any of the permissible uses in the Residential and Commercial Zone subject to: i) Premium at the rate of 5% of the land value arrived at as per ASR... ii) NOC from Labour Commissioner for industries not in operation or to be closed... v) In layout of land up to 2 Ha., 10% of land for public utilities; more than 2 Ha., 10% upto 2 Ha. and 15% for area above 2 Ha... xvi) The provision of inclusive housing under Reg. 3.8 shall not be applicable."

REG. 4.8.1(v) // TIERED AMENITY

10% + 15% Surrender Math

Surrender is graduated: exactly 10% for land up to 2.0 Ha, and 15% for incremental land above 2.0 Ha. Surrendered land generates 100% in-situ FSI credit.

REG. 4.8.1(xvi) // EXEMPTION

Inclusive Housing Exemption

Because the developer pays 5% ASR premium and surrenders massive amenity parcels, UDCPR explicitly exempts I-to-R projects from Reg. 3.8 inclusive housing!

04 // WORKED PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Worked Practical Calculation

Practical Scrutiny Problem: An engineering firm owns a closed manufacturing shed on a 30,000 sq.m (3.0 Ha) plot in Pimpri-Chinchwad. Ready Reckoner land rate is ₹15,000 per sq.m. The developer seeks conversion to a residential township.

1. CONVERSION PREMIUM
Total Land Value = $30,000 \times ₹15,000 = ₹45,00,00,000$.
Conversion Premium @ 5% = ₹2,25,00,000.
Split: ₹1,12,50,000 to Municipal Corporation and ₹1,12,50,000 to State Government.
2. AMENITY SURRENDER MATH
• First 2.0 Ha (20,000 sq.m) @ 10% = 2,000 sq.m
• Remaining 1.0 Ha (10,000 sq.m) @ 15% = 1,500 sq.m
• Total Amenity Land Surrendered: $2,000 + 1,500 = \mathbf{3,500\text{ sq.m}}$ handed over free of cost.
• 50% of surrendered land ($1,750\text{ sq.m}$) reserved for public garden.
3. FSI IN-SITU BENEFIT
Remaining buildable land = $30,000 - 3,500 = 26,500\text{ sq.m}$.
In-situ compensatory FSI credit = 3,500 sq.m BUA loaded onto remaining 26,500 sq.m plot area!
✓ STATUTORY RESULT: Conversion approved, ₹2.25 Cr premium paid, 3,500 sqm amenity surrendered with 100% FSI credit.
05 // SCRUTINY WATCHOUTS

Common Pitfalls & Sanction Watchouts

Pitfall 1: Applying for I-to-R Conversion on Textile Mill Lands

Regulation 4.8.1 explicitly states "excluding the area of Cotton Textile Mills". Applying under this general regulation for mill lands will lead to summary cancellation by the Urban Development Department.

Pitfall 2: Demanding Labour Commissioner NOC on Virgin Raw Industrial Land

Municipal scrutinizers often mechanically demand a Labour Commissioner NOC for all industrial plots. The second sentence of Reg. 4.8.1(b)(ii) clarifies: "However, in respect of any open land in the Industrial Zone where industry never existed, NOC from Labour Commissioner shall not be required."

Amendment & Clarification History (#)
