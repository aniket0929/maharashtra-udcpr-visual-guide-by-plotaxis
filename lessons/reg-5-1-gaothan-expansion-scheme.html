Gaothan Expansion Scheme & Peri-Urban Development Belts

Master the most widely applied land development mechanism in rural and peri-urban Maharashtra: the Gaothan Expansion Scheme under Regulation 5.1.1. Learn how agricultural lands within 500m to 1500m of village settlements or within 2km of municipal limits automatically qualify for residential development upon paying 15% ASR premium, the 50% gut number rule, and exemptions for personal homes.

01 // EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Plain-Language Executive Summary

In rural Maharashtra, vast stretches of land are not covered by city Development Plans, but by overarching Regional Plans (RP). Often, villages grow faster than static statutory plans. Regulation 5.1.1 allows natural residential development on agricultural lands immediately adjoining historic village cores (Gaothans) and city municipal limits without waiting for an official plan revision.

1. Statutory Peripheral Expansion Belts (Reg. 5.1.1(i–vi))

Adjoining Municipal Corporation Boundary 2.00 km belt Measured outwards from municipal corporation boundary into RP area. Guided by road network.
Adjoining Municipal Council Boundary 1.00 km belt Measured outwards from municipal council municipal limits.
Adjoining Nagar Panchayat Boundary 0.50 km (500m) Measured outwards from Nagar Panchayat boundary.
Village Gaothan ≤ 5,000 Population 500 meters From defined gaothan limits based on latest Census.
Village Gaothan > 5,000 Population 1,500 meters Expanded peri-urban radius for larger rural growth centers.
MMR & Raigad Regional Plan Villages 500 meters Capped at 500m regardless of population.
Western Ghats Eco-Sensitive Hilly Districts 200 meters only Pune, Satara, Kolhapur, Ahmednagar, Sindhudurg, etc. strictly capped for ecological preservation.

2. Premium Calculation & Statutory Exemptions

  • Standard Premium: Development in gaothan expansion belts requires paying a conversion premium of 15% of the land rate prescribed in the prevailing Annual Statement of Rates (ASR / Ready Reckoner).
  • Reduced 5% Rate: For areas converted into Municipal Councils / Nagar Panchayats, the premium rate drops to 5% of the ASR rate.
  • Exemption for Individual Houses: No premium is payable for developing an individual residence of an owner or a farmhouse on the owner's land.
  • Exemption for Committed Permissions: No premium is charged for revised permissions where layout approval was granted prior to the sanction of the Regional Plan.

3. The 50% Gut / Survey Number Rule

Contiguity Relief: Where more than 50 percent of the area of a single Survey Number or Gut Number falls inside the statutory peripheral expansion belt, the entire remainder of that Survey / Gut Number within the same ownership is deemed eligible for residential development upon payment of premium!

Additionally, distance can be measured from the nearest gaothan of any village, and applies to all declared/notified gaothans under the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code (MLRC) whether shown on the Regional Plan map or not.

02 // STATUTORY RULE

Statutory Rule & Clause Breakdown

UDCPR Reg. 5.1.1 STATUTORY PROVISION

"5.1.1 Development Permissible Adjacent to Gaothan: For the villages in the area of Regional Plans... where no specific residential zone is shown, development permissible in residential zone may be permitted: i) within 2.0 km from boundaries of Municipal Corporation, 1.0 km from Municipal Council, 0.5 km from Nagar Panchayat... ii) within 500 m. from gaothan limits of settlements <= 5000 population... iii) within 1500 m. from gaothan limits of settlements > 5000 population... v) in Western Ghats eco-sensitive hilly area such distance shall be 200 m. only... on payment of premium considering 15% rate of the said land as prescribed in ASR... no such premium shall be applicable for individual house of an owner."

REG. 5.1.1 // PERIPHERAL BELTS

500m vs 1,500m Radii

Census population governs: villages ≤ 5,000 get 500m belt; larger villages get 1,500m. 2.0 km applies to lands bordering Municipal Corporations.

REG. 5.1.1 // 50% GUT RULE

Whole Gut Inclusion

If more than half of a gut parcel intersects the boundary arc, the developer can develop 100% of the gut without chopping the layout in two!

04 // WORKED PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Worked Practical Calculation

Practical Scrutiny Problem: A developer purchases an agricultural land parcel in Gut No. 142 measuring 12,000 sq.m in a village of 7,200 population under the Pune Regional Plan. Ready Reckoner rate is ₹4,000 / sq.m. The certified Mojni plan shows that the 1,500m gaothan buffer arc covers 7,000 sq.m of the parcel.

50% GUT RULE CHECK
Total parcel area = 12,000 sq.m.
Area inside 1,500m arc = 7,000 sq.m ($58.3\%$).
Because $58.3\% > 50\%$, under the 4th proviso of Regulation 5.1.1: "Where more than 50 percent of area... is covered within the peripheral distance, the remaining whole of such Gut Number... shall be considered for development"!
Eligible Residential Area: All 12,000 sq.m is eligible for residential layout sanction!
PREMIUM COMPUTATION
Total Land Value = $12,000 \times ₹4,000 = ₹4,80,00,000$.
Gaothan Expansion Premium @ 15% = $₹4,80,00,000 \times 15\% = \mathbf{₹72,00,000}$.
✓ STATUTORY RESULT: Entire 12,000 sq.m approved for residential development upon payment of ₹72 Lakhs premium.
05 // SCRUTINY WATCHOUTS

Common Pitfalls & Sanction Watchouts

Pitfall 1: Assuming Western Ghats Villages Have 500m Expansion Belts

In eco-sensitive talukas of Satara, Pune, Kolhapur, and Ahmednagar, Regulation 5.1.1(v) restricts the gaothan expansion belt strictly to 200 meters only. Applying for a residential layout at 350m will result in outright rejection.

Pitfall 2: Demanding 15% Premium on Farmer's Personal Residence

Revenue and collectorate desks often mistakenly issue premium demand notices to farmers building their personal residential home. Proviso 3 of Reg. 5.1.1 explicitly dictates: "no such premium shall be applicable for development of an individual house of an owner".
