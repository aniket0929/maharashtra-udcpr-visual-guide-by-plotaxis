Road Widening Surrender & Staff Quarters FSI

Master the two foundational public land-acquisition mechanisms in UDCPR: how private owners monetize road widenings through in-situ FSI or TDR, and how government agencies build high-density staff housing at zero premium.

01 // EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Plain-Language Executive Summary

Urban expansion requires vast road networks and staff housing for essential public servants (police personnel, municipal workers, revenue officers). UDCPR addresses these needs through two statutory mechanisms: Regulation 7.2 for road surrender compensation, and Regulation 7.3 for high-density staff quarters.

Under Regulation 7.2, when a landowner surrenders land affected by DP road widenings, new classified highways, or DP reservations free of cost and encumbrances, they receive compensatory FSI. This FSI can either be consumed directly on the remaining balance land (as in-situ FSI within Table 6-G potential) or converted into market-tradable Transferable Development Rights (TDR) under Chapter 11.

Under Regulation 7.3, the State incentivizes public employee housing. On lands owned by government bodies or municipal corporations, projects executed by PWD, MHADA, Maharashtra Police Housing Corporation, or 51% State-owned SPVs can consume an elevated FSI of 2.50 to 3.00.

To finance these massive projects, up to one-third (1/3rd) of total permissible FSI may be developed as a Free Sale Component (residential flats or commercial space sold on the open market). Best of all, these projects are legally exempt from all municipal scrutiny fees, development premiums, and Inclusive Housing surrender obligations!

COMPENSATORY CREDIT

100% Road Surrender FSI (Reg 7.2)

Surrendering unencumbered land for DP roads or service roads generates compensatory FSI equal to the surrendered area in accordance with Chapter 11 TDR norms. The owner can consume this in-situ on the retained plot or convert it into negotiable TDR script.

CIVIC EMPLOYEE HOUSING

Staff Housing FSI Up to 3.00

Under Reg 7.3.3, staff quarters projects on plots ≥ 4,000 sq.m fronting roads ≥ 18.0m are granted an FSI of 3.00 (2.50 FSI on 12.0m roads). Calculated on gross area without deducting staff quarters reservations.

FINANCIAL VIABILITY

1/3rd Free Sale Component

To self-fund construction, the implementing agency can build up to 33.33% (1/3rd) of total BUA as free-sale residential or commercial space. Priority offer must be given to Central/State PSUs before open market disposal.

TOTAL FISCAL EXEMPTION

Zero Premium & Zero Fees

Regulation 7.3.6 grants total immunity: NIL premium, NIL scrutiny fees, and total exemption from the 20% Inclusive Housing quota under Regulation 3.8, speeding up execution for police and municipal housing.

04 // WORKED PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Worked Practical Calculation

Worked Case: Structuring a Self-Financing Police Housing Project under Reg 7.3

Project Parameters:

  • Plot Location: Land belonging to Maharashtra Police Housing Corporation in Thane.
  • Plot Area = 6,000 sq.m on a 24.0-meter DP road.
  • Implementing Agency: Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) with 51% GoM equity.

Step 1: Gross Permissible Built-Up Area

Because plot is $\ge 4,000\text{ sq.m}$ and road is $\ge 18.0\text{m}$, permissible FSI = 3.00: $$\text{Total Permissible BUA} = 6,000\text{ sq.m} \times 3.00 = \mathbf{18,000\text{ sq.m}}$$

Step 2: Cross-Subsidization Allocation (1/3rd Free Sale Component)

Under Reg 7.3.5(ii-a), up to 1/3rd of total FSI is permitted for free sale: $$\text{Free Sale Component (1/3rd)} = \frac{18,000}{3} = \mathbf{6,000\text{ sq.m}}$$ $$\text{Police Staff Quarters (2/3rds)} = 18,000 - 6,000 = \mathbf{12,000\text{ sq.m}}$$

If each police quarter has a carpet area of 50 sq.m (approx 65 sq.m BUA), this project yields: $$\text{Total Police Homes Built} = \frac{12,000}{65} \approx \mathbf{184\text{ Modern Quarters}}$$

Step 3: Statutory Fee Exemptions (Reg 7.3.6)

• Scrutiny Fees: Rs 0 (Completely Waived).

• Premium for 3.0 FSI: Rs 0 (Completely Waived).

• Inclusive Housing Obligation: 0 sq.m (Exempt from surrendering 20% EWS housing to MHADA).

05 // SCRUTINY WATCHOUTS

Common Pitfalls & Sanction Watchouts

OPERATIONAL & LEGAL PITFALLS IN ROAD & STAFF HOUSING SCHEMES

  • Claiming Reg 7.2 FSI in Town Planning Schemes (TPS): Regulation 7.2 explicitly states that road surrender concessions do not apply to roads in Town Planning Scheme areas, because road reconstitution is already resolved within the TPS final scheme.
  • Exceeding 1/3rd Free Sale Limit: The free sale component under Regulation 7.3 can never exceed 33.33% of the total consumed FSI. Over-allocating to market sale will halt building permissions.
  • Failing to Record City Survey Transfer: After surrendering road widening strips under Reg 7.2, the owner must ensure the land is formally mutated into the name of the Planning Authority in the City Survey / 7/12 record before full Occupancy Certificate is released.
