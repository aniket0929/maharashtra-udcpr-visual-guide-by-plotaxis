Chapter 6: General Building Requirements - Setback, Marginal Distance, Height and Permissible FSI

The statutory core of Maharashtra architecture: Master the FSI Stacking Pyramid (Basic, 35% Premium, TDR loading), the unified 60%/80% Ancillary FSI allowance, road-width driven front setbacks, the H/5 high-rise margin formula, 6.0m clear fire driveways, and vertical height limitations.

STATUTORY HIGHLIGHTS

Key Chapter Takeaways

  • FSI Stacking Architecture (Tables 6-A & 6-G): Basic FSI (1.10 non-congested / 1.50–2.00 congested), Premium FSI @ 35% unguided ASR land rate, and TDR Loading with mandatory 30%–50% Slum/URT/Amenity sub-quota.
  • Note (xiv) Base Rule: Premium FSI + TDR potential is calculated on the gross plot area before DP road surrender, whereas Basic FSI is calculated strictly on the net retained plot.
  • Ancillary Area FSI (Reg. 6.3 Note i): 60% for residential, 80% for non-residential on payment of 10%–15% ASR land rate. Measured floor-by-floor along outer P-line (Reg. 6.6), abolishing old free-of-FSI ambiguities.
  • Front Setback Invariance (Reg. 6.2.3a): Front setbacks are determined strictly by abutting road width (Table 6-D) and never increase with building height.
  • The H/5 High-Rise Formula (Reg. 6.2.3b): Side and rear margins equal $H/5$ above low-rise thresholds, subject to a statutory maximum ceiling of 12.0 meters from the plot boundary. Parking floors up to 6.0m are deducted from $H$.
  • Building Separation & Step Margins (Reg. 6.2.3c & 6.2.4): Spacing between two towers in a layout equals the margin of the taller building. Ground level margin can never drop below 6.0m for special buildings.
  • Exclusions & Fire Driveways (Reg. 6.7 & 6.8): 100% FSI exclusion for parking stilts, basements, podiums, service floors (≤ 1.8m), and refuge areas. Continuous 6.0m clear motorable driveway required around Special Buildings (≥ 15m height).
  • Height Caps & Chowks (Reg. 6.9 & 6.10): Uncapped height with CFO NOC in metro hubs; 70m/50m caps elsewhere. Mandatory ≥ 12.0m road for heights > 24.0m. Zero-FSI recreational floors (Reg. 6.14) and Hirkani Kaksha ladies room (Reg. 6.15).

Point Access & Dead-End Roads: Building Potential vs Building Height

❓ The Practical Ambiguity / Dispute

Table 6-G Note (xii) allows plots accessed via a dead-end road (point access) <= 100m in length to enjoy the full FSI potential of the main road. Architects assumed this meant they could also build high-rise towers up to unlimited height, ignoring access widths required for fire engines.

⚖️ Statutory Govt Ruling (Sec. 154 MRTP)

The Government ruled that while FSI potential is derived from the main road, building height is NOT unrestricted. Permissions must jointly comply with Reg 3.3.9 (Special Building access width), Reg 6.10.1 (general height limitations), and for Pune Municipal Corporation, Reg 10.1.1 specific height ceilings. Fire tender maneuverability cannot be compromised.

📐 Practical Takeaway for Architects & Students

You can get high FSI from the arterial road on a short cul-de-sac (<=100m), but your structure's vertical height is still strictly bounded by physical access width and CFO fire tender road norms.

Gross vs Net Plot Area for Calculating Premium FSI & TDR Potential

❓ The Practical Ambiguity / Dispute

When a plot loses land to DP road widening, public reservations, or mandatory amenity space handed over to the Authority, should Premium FSI and TDR loading caps be calculated on the original gross plot area or the net balance plot area?

⚖️ Statutory Govt Ruling (Sec. 154 MRTP)

The Government clarified that Basic FSI is calculated strictly on the net plot remaining with the owner after deducting surrendered areas. However, the ENTIRE gross area of the plot may be considered for calculating permissible Premium FSI + TDR potential, provided the reservation or amenity space was handed over to the Authority.

📐 Practical Takeaway for Architects & Students

Land surrendered for public amenities or road widening is not a total FSI loss: the owner retains the legal right to load Premium FSI and TDR over the surrendered area onto their remaining net land parcel.

Front Margins for Special Buildings vs High-Rise Height Formulas

❓ The Practical Ambiguity / Dispute

For buildings taller than standard height, Reg 6.2.3 requires increasing margins. Municipal officers were demanding that the front road setback also be stepped back proportionally to building height, breaking street building lines.

⚖️ Statutory Govt Ruling (Sec. 154 MRTP)

The Government clarified that for Special Buildings, front margin shall be strictly as specified in Table 6-D IRRESPECTIVE of building height to maintain a uniform street line. However, side and rear marginal distances must strictly provide a minimum 6.0m clear motorable access way for fire appliances as per Reg 2.2.8(a) and Reg 3.3.9.

📐 Practical Takeaway for Architects & Students

Front setbacks follow the road width table uniformly so your facade aligns with the street; side and rear setbacks must maintain the mandatory 6.00m unobstructed fire-fighting ring corridor.

Exemption of Stilt Parking Height When Housing Society Amenities

❓ The Practical Ambiguity / Dispute

Reg 6.2.3(b) allows stilt parking height up to 6.0m to be deducted when calculating overall building height for marginal distances. Scrutiny officers objected when developers placed mandatory society amenities (fitness center, crèche, society office, driver's room under Reg 9.31) in part of the stilt, claiming the stilt was no longer purely parking.

⚖️ Statutory Govt Ruling (Sec. 154 MRTP)

The Government clarified that amenities permitted under Regulation 9.31(i) to (iii) located within part of the parking stilt are considered ancillary to parking. Therefore, stilt height up to 6.0m continues to be excluded when measuring building height for marginal distances.

📐 Practical Takeaway for Architects & Students

Locating society offices, gyms, driver restrooms, or daycare rooms in the stilt floor under Reg 9.31 does not disqualify the 6m height exemption for marginal distance calculations.
